METHODOLOGY

How the Strategic World Brief works

The aim is an integrated, high-level understanding of world developments that gives insight rather than a random collection of facts. The brief tracks 20 ongoing storylines and files each day's developments under the storyline they move.

How it is made

  • Broad, balanced inputs. Each day the brief draws on a broad, politically balanced mix of international news and expert commentary from across the spectrum and around the world.
  • Processed daily. Software groups related reports into events, files them under storylines and ranks them by significance. A language model then writes the analysis from that shortlist.
  • Claims checked against sources. Every claim must cite its source, and each citation is checked automatically. Anything without a valid source is dropped before publishing. Each claim links to its source so readers can check it.
  • Coverage-balance checks. Events covered from only one part of the spectrum are flagged with a One-sided coverage badge and treated with extra caution. Facts are kept separate from interpretation, and loaded language is avoided.

Accountability

The brief describes and analyses; it does not forecast. History is shaped by contingency and wildcard actors, so each thread sets out the larger context, the longer history, what each party has at stake, the tensions pulling against each other, the current direction of observable momentum and the questions that remain unresolved.

Limits

This is machine-assisted analysis. Automated grouping sometimes makes mistakes, so check the linked sources before relying on any single item.
