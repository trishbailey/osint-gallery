The forces behind the storylines

Great-power realignment

Russia, Europe, the United States and China are contesting who sets the terms for Europe's security and Ukraine's war.

Evidence now. Finland's and Norway's leaders called Putin directly, Zelensky warned against a new Yalta, and Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa and partner economies bloc (BRICS) parliaments backed United Nations (UN) Security Council reform as China took the forum's chair.

Counterview. The Nordic leaders say they spoke only for themselves and nothing moved, so these contacts may not change alignments.

The tensions.

  • Direct diplomacy vs alliance unity: Frontline states open their own channels to Moscow, while Washington leads the formal talks.

What's unresolved. It is unresolved whether a United States (US)-China-Russia meeting takes place and whether Ukraine is at the table first.

Energy-driven monetary squeeze

The Iran war keeps an oil risk premium in prices, which keeps inflation above target and rates restrictive.

Evidence now. Brent rose to $115.47 after the claimed Ras Tanura strike, US inflation is 3.7%, and the euro is near its yearly low before the European Central Bank (ECB) meets.

Counterview. ING says part of the oil price rests on bets about US strikes after the midterms, a premium that can reverse.

The tensions.

  • Inflation vs growth: Energy costs push central banks to hold rates high while voters feel prices first.

What's unresolved. It is unresolved how long Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia keep a risk premium in oil.

Electoral anger meets institutions

Anger at prices, security failures and incumbents drives votes, while courts, election officials and coalition rules decide what that anger can change.

Evidence now. Israel's vote left both blocs short of 61 seats, Brazil's defeated candidate left for Miami despite a court order, and the FEC filing system went offline a week before the midterms.

Counterview. Brazil's incumbent won and Bolsonaro's party accepted the count, so institutions absorbed the strongest challenge this week.

The tensions.

  • Popular anger vs institutional checks: Voters punish leaders, while courts and election bodies absorb challenges to the count.

What's unresolved. It is unresolved whether Israel's coalition talks include Arab-led parties and when the FEC restores filing.

What voters are angry about

Grievances are described as voters state them in surveys. Every figure is cited; gaps are listed rather than filled. As of 2026-10-25.

Cost of living and inflation

Prices, energy bills and wages that do not keep up are the most-cited worry across rich and middle-income democracies.

  • Inflation top global concern for 17 straight months (30 countries, ~20,000 adults) (2026-09) G1
  • European Union (EU): rising prices/cost of living named top issue by 31%; economic situation 19% (2026-03) G4
  • US: economy 'very important' to 84% of registered voters (R 82%, D 88%) (2026-10) G10
  • Brazil: over two-thirds say purchasing power fell vs a year earlier (Quaest) (2026-08) G18

Who channels it: left: Lula's Workers' Party campaigns as defender of wages and social programmes, but trails on cost of living; right: AfD courts industrial workers 'who fear for their economic future', US Republicans and Democrats both rank the economy first

Migration and border control

Voters asking for tighter borders describe it as control, fairness and public services under strain.

  • EU: immigration named top issue by 15% (+1 pp) (2026-03) G4
  • US: immigration very important to 59% (R 72%, D 53%) (2026-10) G10
  • Spain: 82.2% want stronger borders after the July Ceuta crossing; Vox 16.6% (Centre for Sociological Research (CIS)) (2026-09) G25

Who channels it: right: Vox in Spain, Reform United Kingdom (UK) and Restore Britain in Britain, AfD in Germany, 28% in Ipsos polling

Corruption and elite distrust

A sense that the system serves insiders and cannot fix itself.

  • Median 64% dissatisfied with how democracy works in 12 high-income countries (35% satisfied) (2025-06) G8
  • US: 69% dissatisfied with democracy (2026-06) G9
  • US: 8 in 10 want substantial change or upheaval in how the country is run (2026-10) G11
  • Brazil: corruption named biggest concern by 20% (Quaest) (2026-09) G17

Who channels it: other: Serbia's Student List, an anti-corruption movement saying little about foreign policy, Bulgaria's Gen Z protesters and Progressive Bulgaria's anti-establishment landslide

War, security and defence costs

Fear of wars near home and of crime at home.

  • Europeans' top worries: wars near EU borders and disinformation (2026-02) G6
  • Poland: concern about military conflict up 9 pp in a month (2026-08) G3
  • Brazil: violence named biggest concern by 31% (2026-09) G17
  • Crime/violence top concern in Peru 59%, Mexico 56%, Sweden 54%, Chile 53%, Brazil 52% (2026-08) G2

Who channels it: right: Flávio Bolsonaro campaigns on crime and security

Economic insecurity and the next generation

Doubt that children will do better than their parents.

  • Only 32% across 28 countries say the next generation will be better off (2026-01) G7
  • US rural voters: 57% name an economic concern (costs, jobs, wages, housing) as the biggest local issue (2026-09) G12

Who channels it: right: AfD among skilled industrial workers

Democracy and institutions

Worry about the health of democracy itself, voiced differently by each side.

  • US: future of democracy very important to 73% (D 85%, R 69%) (2026-10) G10
  • Latin America: support for democracy 52%; 53% accept a non-democratic government if it solves problems (Latinobarómetro 2024) (2024) G20
  • Dissatisfaction with democracy: Japan 76%, South Korea 71% (Pew) (2025) G26

Who channels it: left: US Democratic voters rank democracy second (85%)

Technology decoupling

Control of chips, compute and artificial intelligence (AI) models has become a lever of state power on both sides.

Evidence now. China's draft rules add 23 AI technologies to its export control list, and Chinese AI stocks have lost trillions of dollars in value in recent months.

Counterview. Analysts say the Chinese rules codify what firms already do, so the practical change may be small.

The tensions.

  • Security controls vs commercial reach: Beijing wants to sell AI access abroad while keeping the underlying models and expertise at home.

What's unresolved. It is unresolved when Beijing adopts the draft rules and how it enforces approvals for overseas deployments.
