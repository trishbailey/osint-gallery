Home News War Almost half of the foreign prisoners of war knew they were going to fight against Ukraine – Coordination Headquarters Almost half of the foreign prisoners of war knew they were going to fight against Ukraine – Coordination Headquarters Almost 47% of the foreign prisoners of war surveyed went to fight for Russia in order to obtain Russian citizenship Olha Katsimon News editor at LIGA.net October 19, 17:02 Dmytro Usov (Photo: screenshot from a video by the Media Initiative for Human Rights) Almost half of the foreign citizens who fought on Russia's side and were taken prisoner by Ukraine knew they were going to fight against Ukraine. This was reported... reported Dmytro Usov, Secretary of the Coordination Headquarters for the Treatment of Prisoners of War, at the international conference "Global Crimea: Understanding Ukraine through the South." Read also "Promised a job, sent to the slaughter": How Russia deceptively recruits Africans for the war – NYT Based on the results of the survey of foreign prisoners of war: Disable ads Disable ads → 49.2% of respondents knew they were going to Ukraine and would participate in combat operations. → 28% of respondents did not know where they were going. → 14.8% were unsure → 13.8% were not informed at all about where they were being sent or on whose side they would be fighting. Disable ads Dmytro Usov during the presentation of the survey (Photo: screenshot from a Media Initiative for Human Rights video) Another nearly 47% of foreigners signed contracts with the Russian Ministry of Defense to obtain Russian citizenship or due to promises that it would be granted. More than 79% of those surveyed stated that they wanted to return to Russia rather than to their countries of origin. One of the reasons they cited was the desire to receive the money owed to them by the Russian Ministry of Defense for signing the contract and for their military service. Disable ads 48.8% of the foreign military personnel were wounded when taken prisoner by Ukraine. Another 41% of those surveyed reported signing contracts through military commissariats and recruitment centers. Dmytro Usov during the presentation of the survey (Photo: screenshot from a Media Initiative for Human Rights video) For data from the Coordination Headquarters Since the start of the full-scale invasion, citizens of 59 countries have been taken prisoner by Ukraine. The largest numbers among them are from Uzbekistan, Tajikistan, Belarus, Colombia, Kazakhstan, Azerbaijan, and Afghanistan. Since the beginning of the full-scale war, 45,959 citizens from 151 countries and unrecognized territories have joined the Russian army under contract. Russia is recruiting foreigners to replenish losses at the front. The voluntary participation of some foreigners in the war indicates that Russian recruitment is based not only on deception and coercion , but also on financial interest. According to the Coordination Headquarters, the largest increase in the number of new contracts was recorded among citizens: → Indonesia – from eight in 2025 to 283 in the first nine months of 2026, → Peru – from 18 to 503, → Sierra Leone – from 9 to 225 → Burundi – from 30 to 699. The number of recruited citizens has also increased significantly: → Colombia – from 427 to 1,449, → Yemen – from 136 to 772 → Sudan – from 74 to 223. Among the listed countries, the highest number of contracts in 2026 were signed by citizens of Belarus (1,862), Colombia (1,449), and Yemen (772). Dmytro Usov during the presentation of the survey (Photo: screenshot from a Media Initiative for Human Rights video) Russia has almost completely halted the recruitment of new contract soldiers in a number of countries. In 2026, no new contracts were recorded among citizens of China, Sri Lanka, Ghana, Uganda, South Africa, Iran, and Algeria. By comparison, in 2025, 320 Chinese nationals, 264 citizens of Ghana, and 107 citizens of Uganda joined the Russian army. The recruitment of citizens from Nepal (from 859 in 2023 to three in the first nine months of 2026), Cuba (from 608 to three), and India (from 85 in 2024 to one in 2026) has decreased significantly. Citizens of Egypt, Nigeria, Turkey, and Serbia also signed far fewer contracts.
