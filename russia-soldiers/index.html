Remote regions bear the cost

Key

    Named dead per 100,000 by federal district

    Each region: distance from Moscow against named dead per 100,000

    Remote: supported. Far East 389 against 108 in the Central district per 100,000; distance r = 0.64.

    How to read this

    The farther a region lies from Moscow, the more of its residents appear on the named list: across 85 regions the correlation between distance and named dead per 100,000 is r = 0.64 (Spearman 0.58). The Far East has 389 per 100,000 against 108 in the Central district, 3.6 times as many, and Siberia has 269. The North Caucasus (106) is as low as the Central district.

    Distance is measured in a straight line from the middle of Moscow city to the middle of each region's largest land area. Pearson's r measures a straight-line link and runs from −1 to 1, where 0 is no link. Spearman's rho does the same for rankings and is less sensitive to a few extreme regions. These describe the data and do not show cause.

    The named list undercounts. A probate-registry estimate puts Russian military dead at 352,000 by the end of 2025, 1.6 times the 217,808 names on the list when it was made.[24]
    What the list and the estimate leave out

    The list holds 282,993 names, of which 277,353 can be tied to one of the 85 regions drawn here. The other 5,640 come from the Donetsk and Luhansk areas, Baikonur, foreigners and unknown origin, and the list leaves out losses of the Donetsk and Luhansk forces.[1] The estimate comes from the Probate Registry: 261,000 deaths found from excess inheritance cases plus about 90,000 from late registrations, as of mid-2025 and likely to rise. It covers Russian citizens only, men aged 18 to 59, and has no regional split, so regional rates here use confirmed names and not true losses.[24]

        Poverty: a weak link

        Key

          Map key

            Share of residents below the poverty line, 2025

            Poverty rate against named dead per 100,000

            Named dead per 100,000 by poverty quarter (21 or 22 regions each)

            Poverty: weak link. Poorest quarter of regions 250 against 124 in the least poor, but r = 0.20 (Spearman 0.25) and poverty adds 0.5 points to the 41.2% that distance already explains.

            How this was tested, and the wage check

            Poverty is the share of residents with income below the poverty line, Rosstat 2025. The link is positive but weak: Pearson r = 0.20 (p = 0.06), Spearman 0.25 (p = 0.02), and it is no stronger without the two derived regions (r = 0.21 across 83). Poor regions are also remote (poverty against distance, Spearman 0.38), and with distance in the model poverty’s standardised weight falls to 0.07 against 0.63 for distance. The poorest regions split: Ingushetia (24.3% poor) and Chechnya (13.8%) have 51 and 39 named dead per 100,000, among the lowest, while Tuva (18.6%, 695) and Buryatia (14.1%, 566) are among the highest.[27] With distance, poverty and Russian share together on 85 regions, 42% of the variation is explained (standardised weights: distance 0.63, poverty 0.09, Russian share 0.03).

            The poverty rate is income-based and Rosstat changed its method in 2021 and revised earlier years in 2024, so it is a rough guide to deprivation. The Nenets and Khanty-Mansi areas have no published rate, so theirs is the published headcount divided by population (Nenets Autonomous Okrug and Khanty-Mansi AO).[28]

              Ethnic minorities: higher in the republics, but not everywhere

              Key

                Map key

                  Ethnic Russians as a share of residents, 2020 census

                  Russian share against named dead per 100,000

                  Minority: mixed. Republics 237 against 180 elsewhere, but Russian share alone is not linked (r = −0.08, p = 0.47, 85 regions).

                  How this was tested

                  Ethnic minority republics have 237 named dead per 100,000 against 180 elsewhere, and hold 21.0% of the named dead with 16.8% of residents. The regions with fewer than half Russian residents average 205 against 188 elsewhere. But the pattern is uneven: Tuva (695, 9.5% Russian) is highest, while Chechnya (39) and Ingushetia (51) are among the lowest, and the link with Russian share is weak (r = −0.08, p = 0.47).

                  Shares are from the 2020 census as compiled by real-stats.ru,[30] which lists only the largest peoples of each region and has no Russian row for Dagestan. Dagestan is filled from Rosstat's national-composition table: 102,243 Russians among 3,182,054 residents, 3.2%,[31] so all 85 regions are in the test. Fitting distance, wage and Russian share together on 85 regions explains 41% of the variation in rates, and almost all of that comes from distance (standardised weights: distance 0.65, wage −0.02, Russian share −0.02).

                  Signing bonuses against local wages

                  Bar outline

                    Map key

                      Bonus in months of local average wage

                      Bonus: median 21.3 months of local wages across 79 regions; bigger bonuses go with lower death rates (Spearman −0.30).

                      How the bonus is counted

                      A contract soldier gets a federal payment of 400,000 roubles, reported by Janis Kluge and shown on VoenSchet's regional cards,[5] plus a regional payment that each region sets, read from VoenSchet's summary of regional acts.[32] Each bar adds the two and divides by the region's 2025 average monthly wage,[29] so it shows months of local pay. Municipal top-ups and special bonuses for drone troops or reserve units are left out. Kaliningrad, Nizhny Novgorod and Kirov come from a January 2026 aggregator table and may be out of date;[33] Penza's 1,000,000 regional roubles come from a news report.[34] Where no amount could be confirmed, the region is left out (listed under Method).

                      Finding: the bonus is worth a median of 21.3 months of local wages across 79 regions, and the most months are offered in regions with lower wages: Republic of Dagestan (59.8 months), then Republic of Mordovia and Volgograd Oblast. The regions with the biggest bonuses in months have lower death rates, not higher ones (Spearman −0.30 across 79 regions, −0.14 across the 49 firm ones, which is not significant). A bigger bonus relative to pay does not go with more deaths in a region.

                      Method and limits
                      • Rates are named dead divided by the Rosstat population estimate of 1 Jan 2024. Both the list and the population move over time, and the list lags recent months. The 23 Oct 2026 total of 282,993 is the sum of Mediazona's regional pages; a Meduza article of 10 Jul 2026 quoted a main-list figure of 233,033 and the two were not reconciled.[42]
                      • The map covers 85 regions. Names from Donetsk, Luhansk, Baikonur, foreigners and unknown origin have no regional population or wage and are left out of every rate.
                      • Poverty rates are Rosstat 2025 figures for 83 regions and derived for two. Wages are 2025 nominal averages and bonuses are October 2026 rates, so months of wages are slightly overstated where pay has risen since.
                      • Bonus status: "firm" means the regional card says the amount was confirmed. Other bars are outlined because the programme ends on 31 Oct 2026, the card data is more than 30 days old, the amount is from an older table, or it comes from media reports. Sakha's card gives a range of 1.5 to 3 million rubles regionally and the upper end is charted.
