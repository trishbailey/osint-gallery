Russia's Soldiers

Where Russia's troops for the war in Ukraine come from. Data as of 28 Oct 2026. First draft.

The Russians killed in the war in Ukraine come disproportionately from regions far from Moscow. This page counts the dead who can be named, by home region, and tests whether distance, low pay or ethnic-minority status explains the pattern. The count is a named list kept by Mediazona and BBC News Russian, built from relatives' posts, local media and official statements.[1] It is a floor and not a total: the compilers say it "is not exhaustive, as not every death is publicly reported".[1]

The named list undercounts. It holds 282,993 names, of which 277,353 can be tied to one of the 85 regions drawn here. The other 5,640 come from the Donetsk and Luhansk areas, Baikonur, foreigners and unknown origin. The list leaves out losses of the Donetsk and Luhansk forces.[1] Because reporting differs from place to place, a region's rate compares confirmed names, and not true losses.

Remote regions bear the cost

Far Eastern and Siberian regions lose the most per resident; the Central district and the North Caucasus lose the least. Hover or tap a bar or dot for the numbers.

Key

    Named dead per 100,000 by federal district

    Each region: distance from Moscow against named dead per 100,000

    Remote: supported. The farther a region lies from Moscow, the more of its residents appear on the named list: across 85 regions the correlation between distance and named dead per 100,000 is r = 0.64 (Spearman 0.58). The Far East has 389 per 100,000 against 108 in the Central district, 3.6 times as many, and Siberia has 269. The North Caucasus (106) is as low as the Central district.

    Distance is measured in a straight line from the middle of Moscow city to the middle of each region's largest land area. Pearson's r measures a straight-line link and runs from −1 to 1, where 0 is no link. Spearman's rho does the same for rankings and is less sensitive to a few extreme regions. These describe the data and do not show cause.

        Poverty is not the pattern

        Average monthly wage is the proxy for poverty, since no regional poverty-rate table was available. Hover or tap a dot or bar for the numbers.

        Key

          Named dead per 100,000 by wage quarter (21 or 22 regions each)

          Poor: not supported by wages. Wages do not explain it. Regions with higher average wages have slightly higher rates (Spearman 0.30, rising to 0.38 without Moscow and St Petersburg), and the lowest-paid quarter of regions has 181 per 100,000, below the two middle quarters (227 and 216). The highest rates include well-paid remote regions such as Chukotka and Magadan, while the lowest include Moscow city and St Petersburg. Wages are the 2025 average for organisations, from Rosstat.[7] They are not adjusted for local prices.

          Ethnic minorities: higher in the republics, but not everywhere

          Republics are the 21 ethnic republics. A hollow ring marks a republic on the dots. Hover or tap for the numbers.

          Key

            Named dead per 100,000 by region type

            Minority: mixed. Ethnic minority republics have 237 named dead per 100,000 against 180 elsewhere, and hold 21.0% of the named dead with 16.8% of residents. The regions with fewer than half Russian residents average 232 against 188 elsewhere. But the pattern is uneven: Tuva (695, 9.5% Russian) is highest, while Chechnya (39) and Ingushetia (51) are among the lowest, and the link with Russian share is weak (r = −0.13, p = 0.24). Shares are from the 2020 census as compiled by real-stats.ru.[8] That table lists only the largest peoples of each region. For Dagestan it shows no Russian row, so it is left out of the correlation. Counting Dagestan as 4.0% Russian, the highest share it could have, gives r = −0.08. Fitting distance, wage and Russian share together on 84 regions explains 42% of the variation in rates, and almost all of that comes from distance (standardised weights: distance 0.66, wage −0.03, Russian share −0.06).

            Signing bonuses against local wages

            A contract soldier gets a federal payment of 400,000 roubles, reported by Janis Kluge and shown on VoenSchet's regional cards,[5] plus a regional payment that each region sets. The regional amounts are read from VoenSchet's summary of regional acts.[9] Each bar adds the two and divides by the region's 2025 average monthly wage,[7] so it shows how many months of local pay the bonus equals. Municipal top-ups and special bonuses for drone troops or reserve units are left out. Where a region's act could not be read, it is missing.

            Bar outline

              Finding: the bonus is worth a median of 21.7 months of local wages across 75 regions, and the most months are offered in regions with lower wages: Republic of Dagestan (59.8 months), then Republic of Mordovia and Volgograd Oblast. The regions with the biggest bonuses in months have lower death rates, not higher ones (Spearman −0.30 across 75 regions, −0.14 across the 49 firm ones, which is not significant). A bigger bonus relative to pay does not go with more deaths in a region.

              No credible estimate

              • The total number of contract soldiers who have signed since 2022: the official count for 2025 is 422,704 against about 364,000 federal bonuses, and no source covers every year since 2022.[5]
              • The total number of foreigners: Ukraine's list of 45,959 named foreigners is a floor and not an estimate of the total.[14]
              • The total number of prisoners: Wagner's 48,366 and the Defence Ministry's Storm Z units are counted separately, and the only all-in figure is a claim by Ukraine's intelligence service that could not be verified.[13]

              How the named dead joined

              Among the 85 regions, the Mediazona pages label each name by how the man joined.[2]

              Finding: among the 277,353 names in the 85 regions, 96,613 are labelled volunteers, 27,379 prisoners and 20,690 mobilised men. The rest are labelled by arm of service or have no data. Volunteer and prisoner rates both rise with distance from Moscow (Spearman 0.51 and 0.48). A recruitment agency's internal target list, reported by The Insider, includes "residents of economically depressed regions".[16] The same investigation found recruiters paid up to 1.25 million roubles per signed contract.[16] Wagner recruited at least 48,366 prisoners in 2022 and 2023.[11] The North Korean figure is for the Kursk campaign only.[15]

              Method and limits
              • Rates are named dead divided by the Rosstat population estimate of 1 Jan 2024. Both the list and the population move over time, and the list lags recent months.
              • The map covers 85 regions. Names from Donetsk, Luhansk, Baikonur, foreigners and unknown origin have no regional population or wage and are left out of every rate.
              • Wages are 2025 nominal averages and bonuses are October 2026 rates, so months of wages are slightly overstated where pay has risen since.
              • Bonus status: "firm" means the regional card says the amount was confirmed. Other bars are outlined because the programme ends on 31 Oct 2026, the card data is more than 30 days old, or the amount comes from media reports. Sakha's card gives a range of 1.5 to 3 million rubles regionally and the upper end is charted. Karachay-Cherkessia is shown at the standard 2.1 million rubles, and the card gives 3.1 million for reservists.[9]
              • Regions left out of the bonus chart: Kaliningrad Oblast (base payment of 1 million roubles not confirmed by the act text), Chuvash Republic (programme ran only to 30 Sep 2026 with no extension found), Republic of Kalmykia (card does not say whether the 3 million includes the federal payment), Nizhny Novgorod Oblast (regional and municipal payments are reported only as one sum), Republic of Ingushetia (amount depends on the unit and no figure is given), Omsk Oblast (decree ran only to 1 Oct 2026 and no new act was found), Penza Oblast (regional media report 1 million roubles but no act was found), Kirov Oblast (regional amount not confirmed), Udmurt Republic (regional amount not confirmed), Samara Oblast (reported cut from 2.2 million to 400,000 roubles with no act found).
              • Poverty rates by region were not available from Rosstat, so average wages stand in for income. Wages are not adjusted for local prices, so the wage test is only a rough one.
              • Descriptions only. Nothing on this page is a forecast.
