Russia's Soldiers

Who Russia recruits for the war in Ukraine, by type, and who pays the price. Data as of 28 Oct 2026. First draft.

Russia fills its army from several pools: contract soldiers, prisoners, mobilized men and foreigners. Named war dead by home region show who pays.[1]

How to read the yearly chart, with the source of every value

Every row is one estimate per calendar year, taken from the source numbered next to it; the sources differ, so the rows are separate estimates that do not add up to an official total. Each row has its own linear scale from zero. A year with no source is a gap: it is left empty and marked “no data”, and nothing is interpolated or carried over from another year. Contract soldiers and volunteers are official claims, so they may be inflated; independent budget-data estimates are lower and are drawn as a dashed curve with a lighter band (a range for 2024). Mediazona’s later estimate of the 2022 mobilization is a hollow ring. 2026 is part-year, so it is shown as a dot and not joined. <b>Allocation method.</b> Wagner’s total of at least 48,366 prisoners covers 1 July 2022 to 7 February 2023, 222 days.[13][14] At an even pace, 184 days fall in 2022 (40,087) and 38 in 2023 (8,279), which is an allocation, not a count; Wagner’s real pace was uneven. Storm Z’s 28,200 (iStories’ lower bound; the range runs to 29,000)[15] is all placed in 2023 and leaves out the first wave. The prisoners layer for 2023 is the sum. North Korea: the only yearly figure is the 11,000 troops that Western officials estimated in January 2025;[16] later figures are cumulative and are not split by year, so 2025 and 2026 are gaps. Foreign nationals are Ukraine’s yearly counts of contract signers[17] and include Central Asians, whose total is not split by year.[18]

    How the colony map is made, and what it leaves out

    Each Wagner dog tag carried the number of the prison colony the man came from, and the numbers ran in the order the colonies were visited, so the highest number found among a colony’s dead is the fewest men it supplied. Mediazona and BBC News Russian found 501 such numbers, from K1 to K617 with 116 missing, and matched 341 of them to named colonies; locations are as plotted on Mediazona’s map. 160 colonies could not be named, and one more, IK24 Ukhta, is not drawn because Mediazona’s English and Russian labels name different colonies (Ukhta in Komi, and Fornosovo near St Petersburg); together they hold 4,610 of the 48,366 recruits. “Killed” comes from Wagner’s payment records, which cover deaths from January 2022 to August 2023, and is not a loss rate for the recruits.[8]

    Wagner stopped recruiting in prisons on 7 Feb 2023; after that the Defense Ministry took prisoners into Storm Z units, and no colony-level count exists for that period or for the Storm V units that followed.[8][15] Wagner and Storm Z are separate counts by different recruiters, so the chart does not join them.[14] The only all-in figure, 140,000 to 180,000 convicts by Nov 2024, is an unverified claim by Ukraine's intelligence service.[26] Named dead who were prisoners are counted by each man’s home region, not by the colony, and the named list is not exhaustive.[9]

    Signing bonuses against local wages

    Bar outline

      Map key

        Bonus in months of local average wage

        Bonus: median 21.3 months of local wages across 79 regions; bigger bonuses go with lower death rates (Spearman −0.30).

        How the bonus is counted

        A contract soldier gets a federal payment of 400,000 rubles, reported by Janis Kluge and shown on VoenSchet's regional cards,[45] plus a regional payment that each region sets, read from VoenSchet's summary of regional acts.[48] Each bar adds the two and divides by the region's 2025 average monthly wage,[49] so it shows months of local pay. Municipal top-ups and special bonuses for drone troops or reserve units are left out. Kaliningrad, Nizhny Novgorod and Kirov come from a January 2026 aggregator table and may be out of date;[50] Penza's 1,000,000 regional rubles come from a news report.[51] Where no amount could be confirmed, the region is left out (listed under Method).

        Finding: the bonus is worth a median of 21.3 months of local wages across 79 regions, and the most months are offered in regions with lower wages: Republic of Dagestan (59.8 months), then Republic of Mordovia and Volgograd Oblast. The regions with the biggest bonuses in months have lower death rates, not higher ones (Spearman −0.30 across 79 regions, −0.14 across the 49 firm ones, which is not significant). A bigger bonus relative to pay does not go with more deaths in a region.

          How the map is made

          Names are counted by home region from Mediazona's regional pages,[9] and divided by the Rosstat estimate of residents on 1 Jan 2024.[52] Borders are Natural Earth, simplified.[53] Crimea and Sevastopol are drawn as Russian-administered and are not recognized as Russian by Ukraine or most states. Scroll or pinch to zoom, drag to move, tap a region for its details. Regions with no figure are dark blue. Every layer counts men by home region, where they lived, not where a prison, unit or cemetery stands: Mediazona uses the residence in an obituary, then the registration address, then the birthplace, and the burial place only as a last resort.[54] The Prisoners layer is named dead recruited from prison.

          Remote: supported. Far East 389 against 108 in the Central district per 100,000; distance r = 0.64.

          Poverty: weak link. Poorest quarter of regions 250 against 124 in the least poor, but r = 0.20 (Spearman 0.25) and poverty adds 0.5 points to the 41.2% that distance already explains.

          Minority: mixed. Republics 237 against 180 elsewhere, but Russian share alone is not linked (r = −0.08, p = 0.47, 85 regions).

          Wages: no clear pattern. Spearman 0.30; lowest-paid quarter 181, below the two middle quarters (227 and 216).

          How the layers were tested

          The farther a region lies from Moscow, the more of its residents appear on the named list: across 85 regions the correlation between distance and named dead per 100,000 is r = 0.64 (Spearman 0.58). The Far East has 389 per 100,000 against 108 in the Central district, 3.6 times as many, and Siberia has 269. The North Caucasus (106) is as low as the Central district.

          Poverty is the share of residents with income below the poverty line, Rosstat 2025. The link is positive but weak: Pearson r = 0.20 (p = 0.06), Spearman 0.25 (p = 0.02), and it is no stronger without the two derived regions (r = 0.21 across 83). Poor regions are also remote (poverty against distance, Spearman 0.38), and with distance in the model poverty’s standardized weight falls to 0.07 against 0.63 for distance. The poorest regions split: Ingushetia (24.3% poor) and Chechnya (13.8%) have 51 and 39 named dead per 100,000, among the lowest, while Tuva (18.6%, 695) and Buryatia (14.1%, 566) are among the highest.[55] With distance, poverty and Russian share together on 85 regions, 42% of the variation is explained (standardized weights: distance 0.63, poverty 0.09, Russian share 0.03).

          The poverty rate is income-based and Rosstat changed its method in 2021 and revised earlier years in 2024, so it is a rough guide to deprivation. The Nenets and Khanty-Mansi areas have no published rate, so theirs is the published headcount divided by population (Nenets Autonomous Okrug and Khanty-Mansi AO).[56]

          Wages do not explain it. Regions with higher average wages have slightly higher rates (Spearman 0.30, rising to 0.38 without Moscow and St Petersburg), and the lowest-paid quarter of regions has 181 per 100,000, below the two middle quarters (227 and 216). The highest rates include well-paid remote regions such as Chukotka and Magadan, while the lowest include Moscow city and St Petersburg. Wages are the 2025 average for organizations, from Rosstat,[49] not adjusted for local prices.

          Ethnic minority republics have 237 named dead per 100,000 against 180 elsewhere, and hold 21.0% of the named dead with 16.8% of residents. The regions with fewer than half Russian residents average 205 against 188 elsewhere. But the pattern is uneven: Tuva (695, 9.5% Russian) is highest, while Chechnya (39) and Ingushetia (51) are among the lowest, and the link with Russian share is weak (r = −0.08, p = 0.47).

          Shares are from the 2020 census as compiled by real-stats.ru,[57] which lists only the largest peoples of each region and has no Russian row for Dagestan. Dagestan is filled from Rosstat's national-composition table: 102,243 Russians among 3,182,054 residents, 3.2%,[58] so all 85 regions are in the test. Fitting distance, wage and Russian share together on 85 regions explains 41% of the variation in rates, and almost all of that comes from distance (standardized weights: distance 0.65, wage −0.02, Russian share −0.02).

          Distance is measured in a straight line from the middle of Moscow city to the middle of each region's largest land area. Pearson's r measures a straight-line link and runs from −1 to 1, where 0 is no link. Spearman's rho does the same for rankings and is less sensitive to a few extreme regions. These describe the data and do not show cause.

          Named dead by label: 96,613 volunteers, 27,379 prisoners, 20,690 mobilized.

          Named dead by how they joined, cumulative totals

          Counted by each man’s home region. The named list is not exhaustive.[1] One bar per recruit type, linear from zero, sorted largest first, not stacked by year. Russian types are counted by home region (85 regions, Mediazona regional pages, 23 Oct 2026);[9] foreign dead come from BBC News Russian with Mediazona, 10 Jul 2026.[36] Hover a bar for its source.

          The named list undercounts. A probate-registry estimate puts Russian military dead at 352,000 by the end of 2025, 1.6 times the 217,808 names on the list when it was made.[59]
          What the list and the estimate leave out

          The list holds 282,993 names, of which 277,353 can be tied to one of the 85 regions drawn here. The other 5,640 come from the Donetsk and Luhansk areas, Baikonur, foreigners and unknown origin, and the list leaves out losses of the Donetsk and Luhansk forces.[1] The estimate comes from the Probate Registry: 261,000 deaths found from excess inheritance cases plus about 90,000 from late registrations, as of mid-2025 and likely to rise. It covers Russian citizens only, men aged 18 to 59, and has no regional split, so regional rates here use confirmed names and not true losses.[59]

          Method and limits
          • Rates are named dead divided by the Rosstat population estimate of 1 Jan 2024. Both the list and the population move over time, and the list lags recent months. The 23 Oct 2026 total of 282,993 is the sum of Mediazona's regional pages; a Meduza article of 10 Jul 2026 quoted a main-list figure of 233,033 and the two were not reconciled.[39]
          • The map covers 85 regions. Names from Donetsk, Luhansk, Baikonur, foreigners and unknown origin have no regional population or wage and are left out of every rate.
          • Poverty rates are Rosstat 2025 figures for 83 regions and derived for two. Wages are 2025 nominal averages and bonuses are October 2026 rates, so months of wages are slightly overstated where pay has risen since.
          • Bonus status: "firm" means the regional card says the amount was confirmed. Other bars are outlined because the program ends on 31 Oct 2026, the card data is more than 30 days old, the amount is from an older table, or it comes from media reports. Sakha's card gives a range of 1.5 to 3 million rubles regionally and the upper end is charted. Karachay-Cherkessia is shown at the standard 2.1 million rubles, and the card gives 3.1 million for reservists.[48]
          • Regions left out of the bonus chart: Chuvash Republic (program ran only to 30 Sep 2026 with no extension found), Republic of Kalmykia (card does not say whether the 3 million includes the federal payment), Republic of Ingushetia (amount depends on the unit and no figure is given), Omsk Oblast (decree ran only to 1 Oct 2026 and no new act was found), Udmurt Republic (regional amount not confirmed), Samara Oblast (reported cut from 2.2 million to 400,000 rubles with no act found).
          • Descriptions only. Nothing on this page is a forecast.
