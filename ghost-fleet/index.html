The shadow fleet in brief
Why it is called a shadow fleet

Old tankers held by shell companies change names and flags often, sometimes switch off their AIS position beacons, and lack Western insurance.

How it operates

Oil changes hands in ship-to-ship transfers at sea, and ships go quiet near terminals, so where it came from is hard to trace.

What it carries and what risks it brings

It carries Russian crude and fuel to buyers outside the Western price cap, and its age and weak insurance risk spills no insurer would cover.

The Eagle S, which had loaded at a Russian port, cut the Estlink 2 power cable in December 2024; officers are charged with negligence, not sabotage, and an appeal court sent the case back for trial.

Where it goes

Oil loads at Baltic, Black Sea, Arctic and Pacific ports and goes mainly to India, China and Turkey, often after transfers off Greece, Ceuta, Oman and Malaysia.
