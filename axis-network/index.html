How messages travel between Iran's allied armed groups on Telegram

Iran and its allied armed groups run hundreds of Telegram channels. We collected a month of their public posts (20 Sep – 20 Oct 2026) and tracked when the same message appeared on channels belonging to different groups. The map shows which groups pass messages to which.
Groups: Iran's Revolutionary Guard (IRGC) and Iranian state media, Hezbollah, the Houthis, Hamas and Hamas-aligned media, Palestinian Islamic Jihad (PIJ), Iraqi militias, and pro-Axis news outlets such as Al Mayadeen. All times are US Eastern. The graphics play on their own and loop.

What this means

Messages move between these groups every day, mostly from Iranian, Houthi and pro-Axis news outlets toward Hezbollah and Iraqi channels. Most later posts repeat the words without saying where they came from, so readers cannot tell where a claim began. Matching words can also come from a shared statement, so this shows a common supply of material, not proof that one channel copied another.

Public tag: “Bybit 35 (Exchange)”

The wallet sent 188.388 USDT in two transfers, each emptying the balance within 2 minutes of a deposit, to this Bybit-tagged address. That suggests the collection wallet is a Bybit customer deposit address inferred, based on flow behaviour and public tags; Bybit has not confirmed it. 22.54 USDT was still held on 21 Oct.

What this means

A sanctioned media brand advertised a new, unlisted wallet, so the money avoided the addresses already blocked. The sums are small, about $211, but the trail ends at a major exchange, which may hold identity records for the account. The Egyptian mobile-money numbers in the same posts cannot be traced from public data.

Key findings
  • Houthis: half their posts (50%) are about the Yemen–Saudi war, and they denounce the most (22% of posts).
  • Hamas: 83% of posts are about Palestine, and nearly all simply report news (92%).
  • IRGC and Hezbollah: about a third of posts are on the Iran standoff; the IRGC leans hardest on “the enemy is weak” messages (27% of posts).
  • Iraqi militias: the most focused on home affairs, with 27% of posts about domestic politics and security.
  • PIJ: 82% of posts are about martyrs, faith and mobilisation, and 42% are calls to rally.
What this means

The groups share messages, but each keeps its own focus: the Houthis on their war with Saudi Arabia, Hamas on Palestine, the IRGC and Hezbollah on the stand-off with Israel and the US, and the Iraqi militias on politics at home. PIJ stands apart as a mobilising voice rather than a news source. Read alongside the maps above, this shows a network that spreads each other’s content while each member speaks mainly to its own audience.

How this was done: Telegram posts (68,314) were sorted into themes by AI using a written codebook, and a second check agreed with it 85.5% of the time.
