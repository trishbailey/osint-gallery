Defense contractors: who was paid

Counted contracts: $24.7bn across 10,008 awards, in US dollars.

What is not counted

  • NATO PURL packages: NATO does not name the manufacturers, so no company is credited.
  • SIPRI (Stockholm International Peace Research Institute) and Kiel weapons records: these count items delivered, not contract values.
  • Unconfirmed deals: framework agreements with no named customer, replacement orders for donated stocks, contract ceilings, and EU production grants under ASAP (the Act in Support of Ammunition Production).
  • US awards were found by searching contract descriptions for "Ukraine", and the search returns at most 10,000 awards, so some contracts are probably missed.

Notes and unknowns

  • Kiel records US allocations of €115.4bn since February 2022: €64.6bn military, €47.3bn financial and €3.5bn humanitarian. US commitments are €119.0bn. In 2025 Kiel records €0.48bn of new US allocations, all military, and none in 2026 to date.
  • Kiel counts weapons that Europeans buy from US firms for Ukraine, for example through NATO's PURL mechanism, as aid from the European government that pays, not as US aid. The chart below lists PURL contributions as Kiel reports them.
  • G7 ERA (Extraordinary Revenue Acceleration) loans: the US Treasury disbursed USD 20bn to a World Bank fund for Ukraine's budget in December 2024. Kiel does not break the ERA loans out as a separate line in these allocation sheets, so they are not routed separately here. Not broken down by the source.
  • 'All other donors' is calculated as the Kiel total minus the five named donors, using the same sheet. It is the only calculated value.
  • Unknowns: Kiel's monthly financial series ends in June 2026, while the military and humanitarian series run to August 2026. Kiel lists some allocations with an unknown month; these appear only in 'All years'.
  • Source inconsistency: the 'All years' view uses Kiel's Country Summary sheet; the single-year views add up Kiel's monthly sheets. In Release 31 these differ for EU institutions' financial aid (€87.6bn in the summary, €84.4bn in the monthly rows) and for the all-donor totals (military €185.9bn vs 184.7bn; financial 170.7bn vs 170.3bn; humanitarian 26.3bn vs 26.6bn). The US figures match in both sheets. Values are shown as the source reports them, without adjustment.

Accuracy check: 135 of 139 chart values match the Kiel workbook exactly. The other 4 differ because Kiel's own summary and monthly sheets disagree; the charts show the figures as Kiel reports them.
