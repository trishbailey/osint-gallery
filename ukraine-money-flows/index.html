Who funds Ukraine, and with what

Since 2022 the United States is the largest single donor, at EUR 115.4bn allocated, most of it military (EUR 64.6bn). New US allocations nearly stopped in 2025, and European governments and EU institutions now carry the flows.

These charts show aid allocated to Ukraine, meaning delivered or specified for delivery, in the Kiel Institute's own categories (military, financial, humanitarian) and in euros, as the Kiel Institute reports them. Pick a year to see who gave what. Every value is read from a named cell of the Kiel Ukraine Support Tracker, Release 31 (data to 31 August 2026).

Select a year to see how the flows changed

Notes and unknowns

  • Kiel records US allocations of EUR 115.4bn since February 2022: EUR 64.6bn military, EUR 47.3bn financial and EUR 3.5bn humanitarian. US commitments are EUR 119.0bn. In 2025 Kiel records EUR 0.48bn of new US allocations, all military, and none in 2026 to date.
  • Kiel counts weapons that Europeans buy from US firms for Ukraine, for example through NATO's PURL mechanism, as aid from the European government that pays, not as US aid. The chart below lists PURL contributions as Kiel reports them.
  • G7 ERA loans: the US Treasury disbursed USD 20bn to a World Bank fund for Ukraine's budget in December 2024. Kiel does not break the ERA loans out as a separate line in these allocation sheets, so they are not routed separately here. Not broken down by the source.
  • 'All other donors' is calculated as the Kiel total minus the five named donors, using the same sheet. It is the only calculated value.
  • Unknowns: Kiel's monthly financial series ends in June 2026, while the military and humanitarian series run to August 2026. Kiel lists some allocations with an unknown month; these appear only in 'All years'.
  • Source inconsistency: the 'All years' view uses Kiel's Country Summary sheet; the single-year views add up Kiel's monthly sheets. In Release 31 these differ for EU institutions' financial aid (EUR 87.6bn in the summary, EUR 84.4bn in the monthly rows) and for the all-donor totals (military EUR 185.9bn vs 184.7bn; financial 170.7bn vs 170.3bn; humanitarian 26.3bn vs 26.6bn). The US figures match in both sheets. Values are shown as the source reports them, without adjustment.

Accuracy audit

215 of 219 checked values match the source exactly. The 4 that do not are inconsistencies inside Kiel's own workbook: its Country Summary sheet and its monthly sheets give different totals for the same donor and aid type. Both are shown as Kiel reports them, without adjustment.
